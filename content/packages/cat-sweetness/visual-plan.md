# Visual plan - Why Cats Can't Taste Sweetness

**Package:** `cat-sweetness`  
**Format:** 9:16 vertical, 1080x1920, 45 seconds  
**Disclosure:** Illustrative, AI-assisted visuals; not documentary footage.

## Global direction

- **Style:** Warm, tactile food-and-science documentary illustration with macro details and one restrained abstract sequence.
- **Anatomy:** One orange tabby with symmetrical tabby stripes, green eyes, a natural pink tongue, and correct whisker placement. The tiger and cheetah are separate, realistic felines with correct coat patterns.
- **Welfare:** The cat eats plain meat and is never shown eating sugar, chocolate, or unsafe human food. No restraint, fear, or forced interaction.
- **Continuity:** Same cat, kitchen, windowsill, ceramic dishes, and morning light. The abstract sequence keeps the same amber palette and grain.
- **Negative prompt:** No extra limbs, no extra ears, no extra tails, no distorted eyes, no human hands merging with fur, no text artifacts, no logos, no watermarks, no photorealistic claim of archival footage, no distressed animals, no staged aggression.

## Scene-by-scene direction

### 1. s1 (0:00-0:02)

- **Shot:** eye-level close-up
- **Subject:** one orange tabby cat with clear tabby stripes and green eyes
- **Framing:** vertical 9:16, cat's face in the upper middle third, dish blurred at the bottom edge
- **Action:** An orange tabby cat sits on a sunlit kitchen windowsill, looking directly into the lens with a calm, neutral expression. A small ceramic dish of sugar cubes sits far in the foreground, ignored. Soft morning light, realistic fur detail.
- **Camera motion:** slow push-in; the cat blinks once
- **Continuity:** same orange tabby, windowsill, and kitchen in every scene
- **Avoid:** human face, cat eating sugar, distorted eyes, text overlays

### 2. s2 (0:02-0:10)

- **Shot:** low-angle medium shot
- **Subject:** the same orange tabby and a human hand, no visible face
- **Framing:** vertical 9:16, cat and bowl in the lower middle third, clean negative space above
- **Action:** The same orange tabby eats from a shallow bowl of plain shredded meat on a clean kitchen floor. An owner's hand sets the bowl down and withdraws. The cat eats calmly; the sugar dish from the opening is visible but untouched on the counter above.
- **Camera motion:** gentle tilt down from counter to bowl
- **Continuity:** same cat, kitchen, bowl, and sugar dish; no other animals
- **Avoid:** messy food, food aggression, human face, text overlays

### 3. s3 (0:10-0:18)

- **Shot:** macro side close-up
- **Subject:** the same orange tabby's tongue, nose, and whiskers
- **Framing:** vertical 9:16, tongue and water in the lower third, one eye visible above
- **Action:** A macro study of the same orange tabby's face as it laps water from a bowl. The tongue curls naturally, whiskers stay symmetrical, and the nose and eyes remain anatomically correct. Water ripples in slow motion.
- **Camera motion:** high-speed-style slow motion, camera steady
- **Continuity:** same tabby markings and green eye; no collar or harness
- **Avoid:** forked tongue, extra whiskers, human teeth, text overlays

### 4. s4 (0:18-0:26)

- **Shot:** abstract macro animation
- **Subject:** two soft translucent molecular forms, one incomplete
- **Framing:** vertical 9:16, the two forms centered with generous negative space
- **Action:** A restrained abstract animation on a deep amber background: two translucent protein-like forms drift toward each other. One form is complete; the other has a clean gap and dissolves before they can join. No labels, equations, or readable characters appear.
- **Camera motion:** slow orbital drift, then the incomplete form fades
- **Continuity:** same amber palette and grain as the kitchen scenes; no sudden style shift
- **Avoid:** readable text, DNA double helix cliché, medical diagram labels, flashing lights

### 5. s5 (0:26-0:34)

- **Shot:** medium close-up, slight high angle
- **Subject:** the same orange tabby between a sugar dish and a meat bowl
- **Framing:** vertical 9:16, both dishes visible at the lower edges, cat centered
- **Action:** The same orange tabby sniffs a small dish of sugar cubes on the kitchen counter, then turns its head away and steps toward the meat bowl. The cat's movement is unhurried and curious, not disgusted.
- **Camera motion:** single smooth lateral follow as the cat turns away
- **Continuity:** same counter, dishes, and cat; sugar cubes remain uneaten
- **Avoid:** cat eating sugar, knocked-over dishes, human face, text overlays

### 6. s6 (0:34-0:45)

- **Shot:** three static wide portraits, then a close-up
- **Subject:** orange tabby, tiger, and cheetah as separate realistic felines
- **Framing:** vertical 9:16, each animal centered with natural habitat context
- **Action:** A brief, respectful montage of three felines: the same orange tabby on its windowsill, a tiger standing in tall grass, and a cheetah on open savanna. Each shot is calm and observational, then the film returns to the tabby in warm window light.
- **Camera motion:** slow cross-dissolves between the three portraits
- **Continuity:** natural coat patterns, correct feline anatomy, no fantasy hybrids
- **Avoid:** hybrid animals, cages, performing animals, text overlays

## Welfare and authenticity review

- No scene asks for fear, pain, injury, aggression, or staged distress.
- No scene presents generated imagery as archival or documentary footage.
- Species anatomy, coat markings, and body proportions must remain consistent.
- Reject any shot with extra limbs, merged faces, malformed eyes, or text artifacts.
- Keep captions in the lower third and outside the main subject's face.
