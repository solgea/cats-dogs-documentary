# Visual plan - What a Cat's Slow Blink Really Means

**Package:** `slow-blink`  
**Format:** 9:16 vertical, 1080x1920, 45 seconds  
**Disclosure:** Real-footage montage using third-party clips for illustration; not documentary footage or scientific evidence.

## Global direction

- **Style:** Observational real-footage montage built from third-party pet clips, natural light, calm pacing, and species-correct behavior.
- **Anatomy:** Every animal must be species-correct and anatomically coherent. Multiple individuals may appear across clips; reject malformed, uncanny, or merged animals.
- **Welfare:** The cat is calm and free to look away or leave. No restraint, chasing, forced eye contact, or staged fear. The owner's hand stays open and still. Reject any clip with staged distress, restraint, injury, aggression, or a visible watermark.
- **Continuity:** Keep the question, tone, and caption position consistent across clips. Do not imply that different animals are the same individual. Avoid repeated or watermarked clips.
- **Negative prompt:** No extra limbs, no extra ears, no extra tails, no distorted eyes, no malformed or merged animals, no text artifacts, no logos, no watermarks, no social handles, no distressed animals, no staged aggression, no implied documentary evidence.

## Scene-by-scene direction

### 1. s1 (0:00-0:02)

- **Shot:** eye-level close-up
- **Subject:** one gray tabby cat with dark stripes, pale green eyes, and a white chin
- **Framing:** vertical 9:16, the cat's face in the upper middle third, caption-safe lower third
- **Action:** A gray tabby cat sits on a cushioned window seat in a quiet living room, facing the camera with relaxed eyes. The cat performs one slow half-blink, then holds a calm, soft gaze. Morning light falls across its face, and the background is gently out of focus.
- **Camera motion:** slow push-in; the cat half-closes its eyes once
- **Continuity:** same gray tabby, window seat, and warm living room in every scene
- **Avoid:** human face, forced eye contact, startled expression, text overlays

### 2. s2 (0:02-0:10)

- **Shot:** over-the-shoulder two-shot from behind the owner
- **Subject:** the same gray tabby and an owner's relaxed face seen only from the brow down
- **Framing:** vertical 9:16, cat centered, owner's brow and cheek as a soft foreground edge
- **Action:** The same gray tabby rests on the window seat while a seated owner's face stays out of frame. The owner's visible eyes perform a slow blink, and the cat answers with a half-blink of its own. The room is still, and both subjects remain relaxed.
- **Camera motion:** steady camera; one human slow blink, then one cat half-blink
- **Continuity:** same cat, window seat, and oatmeal sweater; no other animals
- **Avoid:** direct stare, human face fully visible, grabbing the cat, text overlays

### 3. s3 (0:10-0:18)

- **Camera motion:** gentle lateral follow as the cat takes two steps forward
- **Continuity:** same cat and living room; the researcher wears a plain blue shirt
- **Avoid:** reaching or grabbing, human face, cornering the cat, text overlays

### 4. s4 (0:18-0:26)

- **Continuity:** same blanket, window seat, and morning light; no new animals

- **Camera motion:** static camera; the cat blinks once and settles
- **Continuity:** same cat, window seat, blanket, and oatmeal sleeve
- **Avoid:** chasing the cat, restraint, human face, text overlays

## Welfare and authenticity review

- No scene asks for fear, pain, injury, aggression, or staged distress.
- Real footage is illustrative B-roll, not evidence; never present a clip as an archival record of the claim.
- Every clip must show species-correct, anatomically coherent animals; reject malformed or uncanny animals.
- Reject watermarks, logos, social handles, burned-in captions, extra limbs, or merged faces.
- Credit every third-party clip source in the YouTube description.
- Keep captions in the lower third and outside the main subject's face.
